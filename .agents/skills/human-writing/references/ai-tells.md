# Formulaic writing signals

Use this list to diagnose generic or mechanical prose. No word or construction proves that text was AI-generated, and removing every listed pattern can make writing worse. Revise only when the usage is vague, promotional, repetitive, unsupported, or less precise than an available alternative.

Those signs were developed for Wikipedia editing and are used here as editorial heuristics, not as an AI-detection method.

## Vocabulary clusters

Review clusters of abstract or promotional terms such as `crucial`, `delve`, `intricate`, `landscape`, `pivotal`, `showcase`, `tapestry`, `testament`, and `vibrant`. Keep a term when it carries the intended technical meaning. Replace it when a concrete fact or direct verb says more.

Common opportunities to simplify include:

- `serves as` or `stands as` when `is` has the same meaning;
- `features` or `offers` when `has` is clearer;
- sentence-opening `Additionally`, `Importantly`, or `Notably` when the relationship is already obvious;
- `seamlessly`, `masterfully`, or similar praise without evidence.

## Promotional framing

Remove claims of importance that the text does not demonstrate:

- `marking a pivotal moment`;
- `a testament to`;
- `highlights the importance of`;
- `reflects broader trends`;
- `renowned for` or `boasts a rich history`;
- a generic legacy, challenges, future outlook, or conclusion section with no task-specific value.

## Mechanical structure

Review these patterns when they recur or force the material into an artificial shape:

- adjective or phrase triplets used for rhythm rather than accuracy;
- `not only X, but also Y` or `it is not about A; it is about B` used as automatic emphasis;
- a fixed introduction, three-part body, and summary regardless of the reader's needs;
- repeated paragraph endings that restate significance;
- uniform sentence lengths and identical list structures.

Keep a three-item list, parallel construction, or conclusion when the content requires it.

## Unsupported analysis

Delete interpretation that does not follow from the stated fact.

```text
Unsupported: The population is 50,000, creating a lively community within its borders.
Supported: The 2025 census recorded a population of 50,000.
```

Replace vague attribution such as `industry reports suggest`, `experts argue`, or `some critics say` with a named source and its actual claim. If no source is available, remove or qualify the statement.

## Word choice and formatting

- Do not force synonyms merely to avoid repeating an exact name or technical term.
- Check `from X to Y` constructions: the endpoints must define a meaningful range.
- Avoid introducing a list or document as a `curated compilation` when a direct description is enough.
- Use boldface, callouts, and headings according to the target document's hierarchy.
- Follow the project's heading capitalization rather than imposing sentence case or title case.
- Link to material that directly helps the reader; omit broad or decorative related links.
- Summarize what a source establishes instead of listing publication names as a substitute for evidence.
